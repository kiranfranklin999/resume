About Me

Kiran Franklin Gnana

Brisbane, Queensland, Australia

Highly passionate Bioinformatics researcher with 3+ years of industry experience in AI-driven omics pipeline development and cloud computing (GCP, HPC). Currently pursuing a PhD in Computational Oncology and Bioinformatics at the QIMR Berghofer Medical Genomics Laboratory / The University of Queensland.

My doctoral research focuses on developing NeoPepFlow, an open-source Nextflow DSL2 pipeline designed for end-to-end tumour antigen discovery from short- and long-read sequencing data. The findings from this pipeline feed directly into PaedNEO-VAX, a personalised paediatric cancer vaccine trial across eight Australian hospitals. By integrating genomics, transcriptomics, and immunogenomics, I aim to improve the accuracy of neoantigen identification to drive personalized, immune-system-driven cancer treatment strategies.

NeoPepFlow (PhD)

  • Leading development of a robust DSL2 Nextflow pipeline supporting short- and long-read RNA-seq and WGS sequencing data.
  • Identifies novel tumor antigens, fusion transcripts (e.g., ZFTA-RELA), and intron retentions.
  • Outputs feed directly into the clinical PaedNEO-VAX trials spanning 8 Australian hospitals.
  • Allows scalable HLA-typing and binding affinity prediction on HPC and GCP platforms.

Paediatric Brain Cancer Antigens

Predicting class I and II neoantigens and tumour-associated antigens across 306 paediatric brain tumours from zero and Dubois cohorts.

Read More

Tumour Antigen Discovery (PhD)

  • Developed a computational workflow to predict class I/II neoantigens and tumour associated antigens (TAAs) in DMG (n=152), MB (n=78), and EPN (n=76) cohorts.
  • Discovered that mismatch-repair deficiency and POLE mutations confer increased predicted immunogenicity even in low-TMB tumours.
  • Identified shared TAA expression profiles (BIRC5, EZHIP, TTK, CAGE1, ZNF560, SMC1B, PRAME) across all paediatric brain cancer subtypes.
  • Established a systematic framework for vaccine eligibility assessment showing that 95.8% of patients are eligible for antigen-directed therapies.
